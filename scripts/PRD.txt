# NutriSnap - Image to Nutrition Calculator PRD

## Overview
NutriSnap is a user-friendly nutrition tracking application that allows users to capture images of their meals and automatically receive nutritional information. Designed for health-conscious individuals who want to monitor their dietary intake without the tedious process of manual logging, NutriSnap uses image recognition technology to identify food items and calculate their nutritional content. The application provides users with valuable insights into their eating habits, helping them make informed decisions about their diet and achieve their health goals.

## Core Features

### 1. Image-Based Food Recognition
- **What it does**: Allows users to take photos of their meals to automatically identify food items
- **Why it's important**: Eliminates the need for manual food logging, making nutrition tracking effortless
- **How it works**: Uses image recognition algorithms and a food database to identify items from photos

### 2. Nutritional Information Display
- **What it does**: Provides comprehensive nutritional breakdown of identified foods (calories, macronutrients, vitamins, minerals)
- **Why it's important**: Gives users immediate insight into the nutritional content of their meals
- **How it works**: Retrieves nutritional data from a database based on the identified food items

### 3. Daily Nutrition Dashboard
- **What it does**: Aggregates nutritional data and displays daily/weekly summaries
- **Why it's important**: Helps users understand their overall dietary patterns and progress
- **How it works**: Compiles data from all logged meals to show total intake and nutritional balance

### 4. Meal History & Tracking
- **What it does**: Stores previous meal entries with images and nutritional information
- **Why it's important**: Enables users to review past consumption and identify patterns
- **How it works**: Maintains a chronological database of user meal entries with search and filter capabilities

### 5. Manual Entry & Correction
- **What it does**: Allows users to manually add or adjust food items and quantities
- **Why it's important**: Provides flexibility when automatic recognition is imprecise or unavailable
- **How it works**: Interface for searching food database and adjusting quantities or nutritional values

## User Experience

### User Personas
1. **Health-Conscious Professional** - Busy individual wanting to maintain healthy eating without spending time on detailed food logging
2. **Fitness Enthusiast** - Active person tracking macros and nutrients to optimize physical performance
3. **Dietary Restriction Manager** - Person with allergies or specific dietary needs monitoring intake of particular nutrients or ingredients

### Key User Flows
1. **Meal Logging Flow**
   - Open app
   - Take photo of meal
   - Review automatically identified foods and adjust if necessary
   - Confirm entry

2. **Nutrition Review Flow**
   - View daily dashboard
   - Check macro breakdown and calorie count
   - Explore detailed nutrient information
   - Compare to previous days/weeks

3. **History Analysis Flow**
   - Access meal history
   - Filter by date/meal type
   - Review patterns and trends
   - Export data if needed

### UI/UX Considerations
- Simple, clean interface prioritizing visual representation of data
- One-tap photo capture for quick meal logging
- Intuitive visualization of nutritional information using charts and graphs
- Color coding to indicate nutritional balance or imbalance
- Minimal text entry required for core functionality

## Risks and Mitigations

### Technical Challenges
1. **Recognition Accuracy**
   - Risk: Poor food identification leading to incorrect nutritional data
   - Mitigation: Implement confidence scores and user verification; improve with machine learning over time

2. **Processing Speed**
   - Risk: Slow image processing creating poor user experience
   - Mitigation: Optimize image handling, implement progressive loading, and provide immediate feedback

3. **Data Reliability**
   - Risk: Incomplete or inaccurate nutritional database
   - Mitigation: Combine multiple data sources, implement regular updates, allow user corrections

### MVP Scoping
1. **Feature Creep**
   - Risk: Expanding scope delays launch of usable product
   - Mitigation: Strict prioritization of features based on user value; phased development approach

2. **User Adoption**
   - Risk: Complex interface discourages regular use
   - Mitigation: Focus on simplicity and immediate value in MVP; gradual introduction of advanced features

### Resource Constraints
1. **Development Complexity**
   - Risk: Limited resources for building advanced recognition capabilities
   - Mitigation: Leverage existing APIs and services initially; develop proprietary solutions over time

2. **Operating Costs**
   - Risk: High costs for image processing and storage
   - Mitigation: Implement efficient image compression, caching strategies, and tiered service model
